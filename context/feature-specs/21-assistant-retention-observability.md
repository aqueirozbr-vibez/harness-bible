## 1. Purpose

Three promises the product makes are kept here. Retention: a reader's questions are text for 30 days and metadata after, and the reader can see that on their account page and in an old conversation. Budget: an organisation's model spend has a hard cap at the gateway, and anyone in it can see how much is used, by whom, and when the cap lands at the current pace, computed from the same rows the gateway wrote. Observability: every model call and every answer leaves a span with the GenAI attributes and no text, the headline metrics (citation rate, flag rate, refusals by cause, first-token p95) are series over the rows the product already keeps, and a secret never reaches a prompt template or a log line, with a scanner in CI whose only acceptable count is zero.

## 3. Retention (35a)

### 3.1 The schedule

Spec 15's `npm run assistant:purge` and spec 08's `npm run auth:sweep` run nightly at 03:10 America/Sao_Paulo. The schedule is code, outside the application: `infra/schedules/README.md` documents the two entries and `infra/schedules/crontab` holds them for a Linux host (`10 3 * * * cd /srv/hb && npm run assistant:purge >> /var/log/hb/purge.log 2>&1`, the sweep at 03:20), with a `schedules.ps1` that registers the same two as Windows scheduled tasks for the development machine (`Register-ScheduledTask` under the user's account, D1). Each run prints its counts and one span line (`kind: "job"`, `job: "assistant:purge"`, counts, ms) that the collector picks up like any other; a run that throws exits 1, and two consecutive failures are the alert of § 5.5. Nothing in the web tier or the workers triggers a purge.

### 3.2 What the reader sees

- `RetentionInstrument` (account page) gains a `.meta` line under the `kv` rows from `conversationStats(user)`: `{n} conversations · oldest text kept until {date}` (the oldest unpurged message's `createdAt + 30 days`) or `no conversations yet`; the rows themselves stay literal.
- A purged assistant turn in the dock, the log and the full page renders `text purged after 30 days · citations and verdicts kept` (`assistant.purged`) in `.nocover` style where the body was, with its chips (citations resolve by `sectionId`, which stays), verdicts and record footer intact; a purged user turn renders the same line in the user block. The log row keeps its mode and verdict and shows `text purged` as its `.small`.
- The conversation list (17) marks a fully purged conversation with `.meta` `text purged`.

### 3.3 What the purge clears in a stored mode result (added 2026-10-03, as built)

Units 32 and 33 store a result on the assistant row so a reload can render it (18 D4, 19 § 6), and some of the model's sentences live there. They are words, so the 30 days take them: a walk's explanations become empty strings (the ordinal and the withheld mark stay), a lab's `reproduces` becomes empty and its `divergingMeans` null, a compare's result becomes `{ openPoint: null }`, a brief's becomes `{ stance }`. The script's inputs, run, values, bars and headline, the lab's facts and figure table and the brief's stance are metadata and stay, which is why a purged walk still shows its strip and bars and a purged lab its table. `purgedModeResult` (`lib/assistant/purge.ts`) is pure and returns the same value, by identity, when nothing of the model's is left in it; the purge writes only when it gets a different value back and counts those writes as `modeResults`. The purge must not compare serialisations: the database returns a JSON column with its keys reordered, and a string comparison rewrites every purged row every night.

A purged walk explanation renders the purged line where the sentence was; a purged compare or brief renders the purged line and its chips through the plain answer body.

Account deletion (08 § 5.8) stays where it is; the purge never deletes a reader's rows. Since unit 62f its research sweep deletes the runs a conversation's delete detached, once the cap's 24 hours have passed and their workflows have ended (feature-specs/45 § 3.5).

## 4. The budget meter (35b)

### 4.1 Queries (`lib/db/budget.ts`, importable by the web tier per 11 § 5.5)

- `monthSpend(principal, month)` (exists): tokens and USD summed over the principal's `BudgetLedger` rows in the month.
- `monthBreakdown(principal, month)`: the month's rows grouped by kind (calls and cost) and the assistant's answers, counted as the distinct `traceId`s of the `ASSISTANT` and `GRADING` rows (D2 as built). `budgetView` (`lib/budget/view.ts`, pure) maps the kinds to the breakdown's consumers: `assistant` (`ASSISTANT`, `GRADING`), `retrieval` (`EMBED`, `RERANK`), `labs` (`LAB`; from Phase 3 by `ref`), each with `tokens` and `share` of the cap. The total is every kind's cost, so the meter agrees with the gateway even for a kind that has no row of its own yet.
- `projection(spend, cap, now)` (pure, `lib/budget/projection.ts`): tokens per day = spend / days elapsed in the month (at least 1); cap date = the day the running total reaches the cap at that pace, when it falls inside the month; else `null`. Arithmetic in code, tested with the screen's numbers (1.84 M used on 21 Sep of a 2.50 M cap → 0.088 M a day → 29 Sep).
- The cap: `Organisation.tokenCap` for members; the individual cap (`INDIVIDUAL_TOKEN_CAP`, 11 D5, a number Andre still owes) for individuals; `null` renders the meter without a cap and the line `no cap set` (§ 8). `tokenCapOf(principal, individualCap)` reads it; the web tier takes `INDIVIDUAL_TOKEN_CAP` from `lib/env.ts` only to print it, and `budgetFacts(principal, now, individualCap)` returns what `budgetView` needs.
- **The unit (D7, as built 2026-10-04).** The gateway does not cap tokens: it caps dollars, the token cap priced at the frontier input rate (11 D3: 1.00 M tokens is a `max_budget` of $5.00). So the meter's `used` is the month's `costUsd` read back at that same rate (`tokensOfUsd` in `lib/gateway/prices.ts`), and percent used is cost over budget, which is what the gateway compares before it refuses. A raw count of tokens would put output (five times the input price), cache reads (a tenth) and self-hosted retrieval (nothing) on one scale and reach 100% at a different moment than the refusal: on the development organisation on 2026-10-04 the raw count is 0.65 M (653,880) and the cost is 0.53 M (529,528) of the 1.00 M cap. The consequence a reader sees: the `Retrieval` row is `0` while the embedder and the re-ranker are self-hosted, and the instrument says why in one line under the table (11 D3: "one formula, stated on the meter").

## 5. Telemetry (35c)

### 5.1 Spans

`lib/telemetry/tracer.ts` initialises the OpenTelemetry Node SDK (`@opentelemetry/sdk-node`, OTLP HTTP exporters) when `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` is set, in the web tier through Next.js's `instrumentation.ts` (`register()`) and in the workers at boot. Without the variable nothing is exported and the JSON lines stay as they are (D3). `logSpan` and the workers' span lines keep writing JSON lines in both cases (Loki reads them); with the SDK on, the same call also ends a real span carrying the line's fields as attributes, so one code path feeds both.

Spans and attributes (architecture § 7's rows, the OTel GenAI semantic conventions for model calls):

| Span | Attributes | Emitted by |
|---|---|---|
| `http.request` | method, route, status, `hb.subject` (id, never e-mail), `hb.policy`, `hb.code` | the handler pipeline (exists as a line) |
| `gen_ai.chat` | `gen_ai.system`, `gen_ai.request.model`, `gen_ai.usage.input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `cache_read_input_tokens`, `cache_creation_input_tokens`, `hb.guide.version`, `hb.guide.sha256`, `hb.prompt.sha256`, `hb.cost_usd`, `hb.purpose`, `hb.principal`, `hb.gateway.call_id`, `hb.first_token_ms` | `callModel` (11 § 5.4) |
| `assistant.retrieve` | `hb.mode`, candidates, `embed_ms`, `sql_ms`, `rerank_ms`, `over_budget`, `vector_ready` | `retrieve()` (12) |
| `assistant.answer` | `hb.message_id`, `hb.mode`, `hb.outcome` (answer · refusal · failed), `hb.refusal.cause`, `hb.citations.total`, `hb.citations.resolved`, `hb.substituted`, `hb.repaired`, `hb.grounded`, `hb.first_token_ms`, `hb.total_ms`, `hb.edition.locale`, `hb.edition.cited`, `hb.edition.mismatch` | the answer flow (13, 15) |
| `aid.run` | aid id, `exit_code`, `timed_out`, `ms`, runner | `runAidForTurn` (18) |
| `job` | job name, counts, ms | the scripts |

No span carries prompt text, answer text, a question, an e-mail or a key (11 § 5.4; § 6 below tests it). `trace_id` on every span is the request's `traceparent` (the pipeline already issues it; the workers continue it).

**As built (35c-2, 2026-10-04).**

- **The packages.** `@opentelemetry/api` 1.9.1, `@opentelemetry/sdk-trace` 2.11.0, `@opentelemetry/resources` 2.11.0 and `@opentelemetry/exporter-trace-otlp-http` 0.222.0 (all Apache-2.0, exact pins, about 25 MB in `node_modules` with what they bring). Not `@opentelemetry/sdk-node`: its tracing exports are deprecated in favour of `sdk-trace`, it brings the gRPC, metrics, logs and Zipkin exporters this product does not use, and its one extra service is the thing this design declines, below.
- **The provider is not the process's global one.** `lib/telemetry/tracer.ts` builds a `TracerProvider` with a batch processor and the OTLP/HTTP exporter and keeps it to itself. Only the spans built from the product's own lines are exported, so a framework's automatic spans, which carry request URLs, cannot reach the collector through it: a search URL carries what the reader typed, and no span may (§ 5.1's last paragraph).
- **A span is built from a finished line.** `lib/telemetry/spans.ts` is the seam (`recordSpan`, no OpenTelemetry import, a no-op until a tracer installs its sink); `lib/telemetry/lines.ts` holds one pure function per line (`httpSpanOf`, `genAiSpanOf`, `jobSpanOf`, `retrieveSpanOf`, `aidRunSpanOf`, `answerSpanOf`) and the three lines this unit adds (`kind: "retrieve"`, `"aid_run"`, `"answer"`, printed by the workers). `logSpan`, `logGatewaySpan` and `logJob` print as before and hand the same line to `recordSpan`. The span's trace id and span id are the line's own (an id generator hands the SDK the ids that are waiting for it), so the ids in the logs, in a response's `traceparent` and in the trace are the same ids; its start is its end minus the line's `ms`.
- **Parents.** A request's span names its caller from the incoming `traceparent` (`SpanLine.parentId`). Everything a request does runs inside `withSpanScope` (the handler pipeline, the workers' server), so a model call, a retrieval, an aid run and the answer are children of the request that made them without a span id being threaded through every call; `workersFetch` sends the request's span id as the parent, which is what joins the workers' spans under the web tier's. A search inside an API request continues the request's trace (`currentTraceId`); a page's loader has no request span and starts its own, as before.
- **Names beyond the table.** The embedder's and the re-ranker's calls go through the same logger as a chat call, so they are spans too: `gen_ai.embeddings` and `gen_ai.rerank`, with `gen_ai.operation.name`. A turn's chat spans are the classifier, the draft (and a repair) under `hb.purpose: assistant` and the grader under `grading`: three or more, not the two § 13.4 counted. `http.request` carries `http.request.method`, `url.path` (the path, never the query string), `http.response.status_code`; retrieval `hb.candidates`, `hb.embed_ms`, `hb.sql_ms`, `hb.rerank_ms`, `hb.over_budget`, `hb.vector_ready`; the aid `hb.aid.id`, `hb.aid.runner`, `hb.aid.exit_code`, `hb.aid.timed_out`; a job `hb.job` and `hb.count.<name>`. `assistant.answer` is emitted by the workers' turn, where the message row is written, for an answer, a refusal and a failed turn (`hb.outcome`, and `hb.code` for the failure); `hb.citations.resolved` is the citations at the release's version (13 § 7.2) and `hb.edition.mismatch` is the edition sensor's own rule (`editionMismatch` in `lib/sensors/edition.ts`), so 35e adds the row and the series, not a second definition. The policy decision stays a line: architecture § 7 sends it to the log store, not to a trace.
- **Nothing a span holds is prose.** A null on a line is an absent attribute. A failed span's status carries the failure's code or the error's name, never its message, because a message can quote the row it failed on; the line keeps the message for the operator. The tests assert, over every string attribute and status of every exported span, that none contains the question, a claim, the reader's e-mail, a key or a token, and that none contains whitespace at all.
- **Where it starts.** The web tier in `instrumentation.ts` (`register`, Node runtime only; `NEXT_RUNTIME` is read there because Next compiles the file for the edge runtime too), the workers in `workers/main.ts` (flushed on `SIGINT` and `SIGTERM`), the two scheduled jobs in their `main` (flushed before they exit). Each says so in one line when it exports. `startTelemetry` loads the OpenTelemetry modules with a dynamic import and only when `OTEL_EXPORTER_OTLP_ENDPOINT` (`lib/env.ts`) is set.
- **One sink per process, on `globalThis`.** Next.js loads `instrumentation.ts` and each route as separate module graphs, so a sink kept in a module variable was installed into a copy no request ever called: the workers exported and the web tier did not. Only the live check showed it, since a test runs one module graph. The sink, the scope and the tracer's handle live on `globalThis`, as the database client does.
- **A collector that fails.** One warning line when exports start failing and one when they succeed again, never one per batch; `recordSpan` cannot throw. An unreachable collector is retried by the exporter in the background for up to its own ten seconds per batch, which no request waits for; a job waits at most that long before it exits.
- **The request mark** (feature-specs/49 § 5.6; unit 79, 2026-10-14). The two request scopes, the handler pipeline's and the workers' server's, pass `request: true` to `withSpanScope`, and `recordSpan` marks a span whose parent it takes from such a scope `hb.parent: request`; a span whose line names its parent, or recorded outside a scope, is not marked. Tempo keeps the parent and the mark, an enumeration; the collector's Langfuse road, which drops every `http.request`, makes the marked span a root there.

### 5.2 The collector and the Grafana stack (`infra/observability/`, compose profile `observe`)

Compose services under `profiles: ["observe"]` (off by default: `docker compose --profile observe up`): `otel-collector` (`otel/opentelemetry-collector-contrib`, `infra/observability/otel-collector.yaml`: OTLP in; traces to Tempo, logs to Loki, the span lines from container stdout through the `filelog` receiver), `tempo`, `loki`, `grafana` (provisioned datasources: Tempo, Loki, and PostgreSQL through a read-only role `hb_metrics` created by `infra/observability/init.sql` with `SELECT` on the activity tables and `BudgetLedger` only; provisioned dashboards from `infra/observability/grafana/dashboards/`). Langfuse stays LiteLLM's callback (11 § 3.6) and is not a compose service in Phase 2: the cost the gate reads is `BudgetLedger`'s (D4). All of it is in region when deployed (architecture § 1); in development it is local.

**As built (35c-3a, 2026-10-04).**

- **Five services under `profiles: ["observe"]`**: `otel-collector` (contrib 0.161.0), `tempo` (3.0.0), `loki` (3.7.8), `grafana` (13.2.3), each pinned by digest with its version beside it, and `observe-init`, which runs once. Started by name, `docker compose --profile observe up -d grafana otel-collector`: a bare `--profile observe up` would start every other service of the file too. Stopped by name as well (`rm -sf …`), because `down` stops the database. The two published ports, the collector's 4318 and Grafana's 3001, are on the loopback alone. `infra/observability/README.md` is the operator's page.
- **The role is granted columns, not tables.** `hb_metrics` may select exactly the columns the statements of `lib/metrics/sql` read, of `Message`, `SensorVerdict`, `Flag`, `Conversation` and `BudgetLedger`: stricter than this section's "`SELECT` on the activity tables", and for a reason. Grafana runs any SQL an editor types, so a table grant on `Message` would put every reader's question one query away. It cannot read `Message.text`, `Citation.claimText`, a mode result, a verdict's detail, an account, a session, a key, the ledger or the book, and it is read-only by default. `tests/observability.test.ts` fails when a statement reads a column that is not granted; `tests/integration/observability.test.ts` runs every statement as the role and attempts fourteen reads and writes it must refuse.
- **`observe-init`, not the database's init scripts.** Those run only on an empty volume; the role has to be creatable on a database that already holds data. `init.sql` is idempotent and holds no password: the service sets it from `HB_METRICS_PASSWORD` (development default `hb_metrics`, as the database's own is `hb`). It also grants `USAGE` on the schema, which a database with a recreated `public` schema gives to nobody but its owner: without it the role is told the tables do not exist, while Grafana's own connection test still passes.
- **The lines come from files, not from container stdout.** The collector's file receiver reads `*.log` under one directory (`HB_LOG_DIR`, default `var/log/hb`, git-ignored), which is where the scheduled jobs already append (`infra/schedules`). In development the web tier and the workers are not containers, and reading every container's log on the machine to find ours is not ours to do; in a deployment where they are containers, the same receiver takes the runtime's log path as a second `include`. Only a line that is JSON with one of the seven kinds the product prints passes; a test compares that list with the code.
- **A line has no time of its own**, so its time in Loki is when it was read. The receiver therefore reads new lines only (`start_at: end`) and keeps its place across a restart of the process. A `ts` on every line would make a late read harmless (tracker Open Questions).
- **Nothing leaves the machine.** Loki's and Tempo's anonymous usage reports and Grafana's reporting, update checks and news feed are switched off; Loki's is on by default, which is why `loki.yaml` exists at all, the rest of it being the image's own development configuration. Traces and lines live in the containers.
- **Langfuse** stays as this section says: LiteLLM's callback, not a service here.
- **Amended by feature-specs/49 (unit 84, 2026-10-21).** Each execution of a workflow's activity is an `activity.<ActivityType>` span, a root of its subject's trace (a research run's stored trace, a suggestion's derived one, or the workflow's), built from the `activity` line the workers' interceptor prints at its end; what the activity records nests under it (feature-specs/49 § 7, § 28).
- **Amended by feature-specs/49 (unit 83, 2026-10-20).** A turn is one tree: its scope, `assistant.answer`, holds its retrieval (itself a scope over the query's embedding and the re-rank), its model calls, its aid's run and a span for each sensor's run, each a `sensor` line; the search page's query is a `search.query` scope with a `search` line. Every product span carries its subject's trace name, tags and release, and the turn's answer its outcome (feature-specs/49 § 6.3, § 6.4, § 8, § 27). Nothing in the seam changes: a span is still built from a finished line.
- **Amended by feature-specs/49 (unit 79, 2026-10-14).** Langfuse is reached through the collector's second traces pipeline, `traces/langfuse`, which keeps only what its two allow-lists name, and it is still not a compose service; the collector alone holds its keys (`infra/observability/.env`), and compose blanks the gateway's Langfuse variables. The Tempo pipeline drops every `hb-gateway` span (`filter/tempo`), so no gateway span reaches Tempo once the gateway exports to the collector (unit 80).

### 5.3 The assistant dashboard (`assistant.json`) and the same series in code

The dashboard "Assistant citation and flag rate" (architecture § 7) is SQL over the rows through the PostgreSQL datasource (D4: no Prometheus in Phase 2; the rows are the truth and the gate reads them too):

| Series | Definition (per day, and the month to date) |
|---|---|
| citation rate | assistant messages of kind answer whose citation verdict is PASS and `substituted = 0`, over answers |
| flag rate | flagged assistant messages over assistant messages; by reason |
| refusal rate by cause | refusals over turns, one series per cause |
| first-token p95 | `percentile_cont(0.95)` of `Message.firstTokenMs` |
| grounded distribution | p50 and p10 of `grounded` |
| cost per active account | month's `BudgetLedger` USD over accounts with ≥ 1 message |
| cache read share | `cache_read_input_tokens` over input tokens on `hb-frontier` calls (the gate's byte-stable prefix check) |
| edition mismatch share | answers with `hb.edition.mismatch` (§ 7) over answers, by locale |
| purge | rows nulled per run, from the job lines |

`lib/metrics/assistant.ts` computes the same series with the same SQL (pure functions over Prisma raw queries, one per series, each returning `{ series, window, points[] }`) and `npm run assistant:metrics -- --month 2026-09` prints them as a table, so the numbers exist without Grafana and the Phase 2 gate (spec 22) reads them from code, not from a dashboard. The dashboard JSON and the code share the SQL text through `lib/metrics/sql/*.sql` files (the provisioning step inlines them; a test asserts the JSON's queries equal the files).

**As built (35c-1, 2026-10-04).** A statement is one file of `lib/metrics/sql/`: a read-only `SELECT` with three parameters (`$1::timestamp` the window's start, `$2::timestamp` its end, exclusive, `$3::text` `day` or `month`; UTC, as the rows are stored) that returns five columns, `bucket, key, n, d, value` (`MetricRowSchema` in `lib/schemas/metrics.ts`). `runSeries` runs it twice, by day for `points[]` and by month for `months[]`: the month to date is the statement's own answer, because a percentile or a count of distinct readers cannot be summed from the days. The dashboard (35c-3) substitutes the three placeholders with Grafana's time macros and a literal, and its test compares the rest of the text with the file. Eight of the nine series are built; the definitions the table left open were decided as follows, each in the file's own header:

- **citation rate**: over the answers that made claims. An answer with no claims to check (citation verdict `NA`: a walk, whose numbers are the script's since guide v1.11) is outside the rate, in the numerator and the denominator; counting it would either punish every walk or pad the rate with answers that cite nothing.
- **flag rate** and **refusal rate by cause**: a line `any` and one line per reason or cause, over every assistant row of the bucket (an answer, a refusal or a failed turn); a flag counts on its message's day.
- **first-token p95**: `percentile_cont(0.95)` as this spec says. Spec 22 P2.5a says nearest-rank and that the two are the same number; they are not between ranks (`percentile_disc` is nearest-rank), so unit 36 chooses one (tracker Open Questions).
- **cost per active account**: the USD of every `BudgetLedger` row of the bucket over the readers with at least one message in it.
- **cache read share**: `cacheReadTokens` over the whole prompt (`inputTokens + cacheReadTokens + cacheWriteTokens`) on `hb-frontier` calls with outcome `ok`; over the uncached input alone the share could pass 100 %.
- **purge**: conversations cleared, by the day of `Conversation.textPurgedAt`. A job line is not a row, so the count of messages a run nulled stays in the job line and is a Loki panel in 35c-3; the row-backed series is what the code and the PostgreSQL datasource can both compute.
- **edition mismatch share** (35e, 2026-10-04, the ninth file): of the answers that cite the book, the share whose reader's language is not the cited edition's, one line per language (`en`, `pt`). An answer that cites nothing is outside it, as it is outside the citation rate. See § 7 as built for what it reads.

`npm run assistant:metrics [-- --month YYYY-MM]` prints every series as a table, the month to date first and each value with what it was computed from (`4 / 10`, `n = 12`); without `--month` it is the current UTC month.

Not collected, by design: time on page, messages per session, per-contributor rankings (architecture § 7); the schema has no such query and the dashboard none.

**As built (35c-3b, 2026-10-04): the dashboard.** `lib/metrics/dashboard.ts` (pure) builds the dashboard and the alert rules from the statements; `npm run observe:generate` writes `infra/observability/grafana/dashboards/assistant.json` and `provisioning/alerting/alerts.json`, and `-- --check` fails when either file is stale. "The provisioning step inlines them" is this generator: a query is the file's text, whole, with `$1::timestamp`, `$2::timestamp` and `$3::text` replaced by `$__timeFrom()::timestamp`, `$__timeTo()::timestamp` and a literal bucket, wrapped only to name the columns a panel wants (`time`, `metric`, a value). The test of § 13.5 is three: the files in the repository are byte for byte what the generator produces; undoing the substitution on any panel's or rule's query gives back a file of `lib/metrics/sql` exactly; and no SQL query in either document is anything else. Thirteen panels: four headline numbers by month (citation rate, flag rate and refusal rate as their `any` line, first-token p95), one panel by day per series in the series' order, and the retention job's own lines from Loki (`{service_name="hb-lines"} | kind="job" | job="assistant:purge"`), which is where the count of messages a run cleared lives. Default range the month to date, UTC, not editable in place (the provider does not keep editor changes: the file is the dashboard).

### 5.4 Alerts (Grafana provisioned rules, `alerts.yaml`)

Secret scanner count > 0 in any log line (§ 6.2's runtime detector, a `hb.secret.detected` attribute) → page; purge job absent for 2 days → warn; first-token p95 > 2 s for an hour → warn; refusal rate by cause > 20 % of turns for a day → warn; citation rate < 90 % for a day → warn. Alert routes are a contact point placeholder in Phase 2 (e-mail in Phase 4, architecture § 2).

**As built (35c-3b, 2026-10-04).** Four rules in one group, generated with the dashboard and provisioned as JSON (`alerts.json`: Grafana's alerting provisioning takes JSON as it takes YAML, and JSON is what a generator without a YAML dependency writes). Each is a warning, with its window in its query and a `math` condition on the result:

- first-token p95 over the last **hour** `> 2000` ms, read as the p95 *of* the hour, not a p95 that stayed high *for* an hour;
- any one refusal cause over the last **day** `> 0.2` of turns (the `any` line is left out: the rule is by cause);
- citation rate over the last **day** `< 0.9`;
- no successful purge line in **two days**: `sum(count_over_time(… | error="" [2d])) < 1`, with no data treated as the alert, so a job that never ran and a job that ran and failed twice both fire it.

An alert passes `'millennium'` as the bucket: wider than any window, so the statement itself computes the window's value (a true percentile, a true ratio) and returns one row per line of the series, which is also what keeps two rows with the same label out of a window that crosses midnight. The group's evaluation interval is Grafana's default, one minute; the thresholds are this section's and are constants in the generator. A window with no turns is `Normal (NoData)` for the three SQL rules. The contact point is Grafana's default, a placeholder address (D6). **The fifth rule arrived with 35d-2**: `a secret was found in a log line`, the only one of severity `page`, on `sum(count_over_time({service_name="hb-lines"} | hb_secret_detected="true" [1d])) > 0` (Loki files the line's `hb.secret.detected` under that name); its look-back is a day, the longest window this section gives a rule, so a finding stays in front of someone until it is a day old, and no such line is `Normal`.

## 6. The secret scanner and redaction (35d)

### 6.1 Templates in CI (`scripts/secret-scan.ts`, `npm run secrets:scan`)

Scans `guides/**` (the prompt templates), `workers/agents/**` and `lib/sensors/**` (the assemblers and the classifier's prompt), `aids/**` and `golden/**` (data the model or its tests read) with a pattern table in `lib/security/secretPatterns.ts`: provider keys (`sk-ant-`, `sk-`, `AKIA`, `ghp_`, `gho_`, `xox[abp]-`, Google `AIza`), bearer tokens, PEM blocks, JWT shapes, connection strings with passwords, and, at runtime only, the literal values of the process's own secret variables (`lib/env.ts`'s secret keys: never the names, the values, compared after loading). The script prints `secrets:scan · {files} files · 0 findings` and exits 1 on any finding, naming file and line without printing the match. CI runs it as its own step after gitleaks (`Secrets in prompt templates and model-visible data (zero findings)`); gitleaks keeps covering the repository as a whole (11 § 8).

**As built (35d-1, 2026-10-04).** `lib/security/secretPatterns.ts` is the one table: ten kinds, each a name and a shape (`pem-block`, `anthropic-key`, `api-key` for the `sk-` family, which the gateway's own virtual keys belong to, `aws-access-key`, `github-token`, `slack-token`, `google-api-key`, `bearer-token`, `jwt`, `connection-string`). A shape is the credential's published format where it has one (`AKIA` and sixteen, `AIza` and thirty-five, a GitHub prefix and thirty-six); where the length is not fixed, the pattern asks for twenty characters after the prefix, the floor that lets a sentence name a prefix without being a finding. A PEM block cut short is found by its first line; a connection string is taken whole, to the end of the word. `lib/security/redact.ts` (pure) has `findSecrets`, which returns kinds, positions and lines and never a match, and `redactSecrets`; an overlap is one finding, the earlier in the text and at the same start the earlier in the table, so `sk-ant-…` is an Anthropic key and `Bearer eyJ…` a bearer token. `lib/security/scan.ts` walks the five folders and `npm run secrets:scan` prints one line per finding on stderr (`guides/assistant/v1.12/prefix.md:14 · anthropic-key`) and the summary on stdout. **Two things beyond the section's text**: a folder that should be scanned and is not there fails the scan, because a scan of nothing is not a pass; and the CI step sits after `npm ci`, which is after gitleaks, since the command needs the dependencies. The literal values of the process's own secrets are a runtime matter and arrive with 35d-2. The tests assemble every example key at run time, so the repository holds no string shaped like a credential for gitleaks, or this scan, to find in its own tests.

### 6.2 Logged text and user text at ingestion (`lib/security/redact.ts`, pure)

`redactSecrets(text) → { text, findings: string[] }` replaces each match with `[redacted:{kind}]` and names the kinds found. Applied:

- to the user's message text before it is stored (`Message.text`) and before it enters the assembler's fence (D5: the model never sees a pasted key either; the reader sees `[redacted:anthropic-key]` in their own turn and the answer is about the rest of the question);
- to every log line and span attribute in `logSpan` and the workers' logger (a defence in depth: no attribute carries text, so a finding here is a defect; it sets `hb.secret.detected = true` for the alert);
- to `Flag` reasons (coded, so nothing to redact; asserted).

Findings count per message goes to the answer span (`hb.secret.redacted`, a number), never the value. Architecture § 9's row is met: the pasted secret is not stored for 30 days, it is redacted at ingestion.

**As built (35d-2, 2026-10-04).**

- **Where the question is redacted: once, in the workers' turn**, before the row is written. `runTurn` calls `redactSecrets(input.text, processSecrets())` first and uses the result for everything after: the `USER` row, the request the flow is given (so the classifier, the fence and the conversation history of later turns), and the ledger record's `questionSha256`, which is the hash of the stored text so a record still matches its row. One place, because the count has to be taken where the redaction happens, and the turn is where the text is both stored and fenced. The web tier forwards the text to the workers over the private channel and stores none of it (the idempotency record holds a hash of the body).
- **The reader's own turn.** `meta` carries `question`, the question as stored, and only when that differs from what was sent (15 § 6.1); the dock shows it in place of what was typed as soon as `meta` arrives, and a reload shows the same from the row. No new string: the mark is the message.
- **Retrieval is given the rest of the question.** The search statement requires every word it is given, and a mark is three words the reader did not write (`redacted`, the provider's name, `key`), which would empty the lexical half or pull it towards sections about keys. `withoutRedactionMarks` strips them for the embedding, the statement and the re-ranker; the model is shown the mark. A question that was nothing but a secret keeps its mark for retrieval, so there is something to ask.
- **The process's own secrets** (§ 6.1's run-time half): `SECRET_ENV_KEYS` beside the schema in `lib/env.ts` names the secret variables; `lib/security/processSecrets.ts` returns their values, eight characters or longer (the schema's own minimum for a secret), longest first, plus what a process registers itself, as the workers do with the gateway's master key. A value found this way is a `process-secret` and outranks a shape that would also match it.
- **The guard on what is written.** `lib/security/guard.ts` walks strings, lists and plain objects. Every JSON line is now printed by one function, `printLine` (`lib/telemetry/print.ts`), which guards it first and adds `hb.secret.detected: true` when the guard replaced something; `recordSpan` guards a span's attributes and status the same way. A test fails if any other code builds a line of its own, and the collector's allow-list is compared with `LINE_KINDS`.
- **`hb.secret.redacted`** is on the answer's line and span for an answer, a refusal and a failed turn: the count for that turn's question.
- **A flag's reason** is one of six codes (`FLAG_REASONS`), asserted to hold nothing the redaction would touch.
- **Verified with one real turn**: a question carrying a made-up key, through the dock, the workers and the provider. The reader's turn showed the mark live and after a reload, the key was in neither the stream nor the page nor either process's log, the row holds the mark, and the model answered the rest of the question with a grounding of 1.00.

## 7. The edition gap while PT editions are pending (35e)

Spec 13's edition sensor passes when every citation resolves in one edition, and sets `citesEdition` when the reader's locale differs. This spec records the gap: the answer span and the message row carry `editionMismatch: boolean` (`locale = "pt"` and `citesEdition = "EN"`), the dashboard's "edition mismatch share" series shows it by locale, and the `cites the EN edition` line stays on the turn (16). When a chapter's PT edition reaches parity (03 D6, the book's open item), retrieval picks it (12 § 6), the mismatch series falls for that chapter, and nothing in the sensor changes. The gate (22) reports the share as information, not a criterion, until parity exists.

**As built (35e, 2026-10-04).** The gap is recorded where the paragraph above says, with one difference: **the message row gains no `editionMismatch` column.** The row already holds the two facts the gap is made of, `Conversation.locale` (the reader's language) and `Message.citesEdition` (the edition the answer cited, written with every answer that cites), and a third column would be a copy that can disagree with its sources and needs a migration to exist. So:

- **the span** carries `hb.edition.locale`, `hb.edition.cited` and `hb.edition.mismatch` (35c-2), by the sensor's own rule (`editionMismatch` in `lib/sensors/edition.ts`);
- **the series** `lib/metrics/sql/edition-mismatch.sql` derives the gap from the two columns (`lower(m."citesEdition") <> c.locale`) and reports it per language, by day and by month; `hb_metrics` is granted `Message.conversationId` and `citesEdition` and `Conversation.id` and `locale` for it, and nothing else new;
- **the dashboard** has its panel, generated like the others; `npm run assistant:metrics` prints it as the eighth of nine;
- **the turn** keeps its `cites the EN edition` line, untouched.

When a chapter's Portuguese edition exists and retrieval picks it, `citesEdition` becomes `PT` for those answers and the `pt` line falls by itself; nothing here changes. If a stored boolean is wanted after all (an index, a report that cannot join), it is one additive migration and a backfill from the same two columns.
